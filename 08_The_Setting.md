# The Setting

## Timeline

* **1957**: The Soviet Union launches the first artificial satellite, Sputnik 1, marking the beginning of the Space Age.

* **1961**: Yuri Gagarin becomes the first human to orbit the Earth aboard Vostok 1\.

* **1969**: The Apollo 11 mission successfully lands humans on Luna, with Neil Armstrong becoming the first person to step foot on its surface.

* **1971**: The Soviet Union's Mars program achieves its first successful Mars landing, signaling the beginning of robotic exploration of the red planet.

* **1970s**: The focus of space exploration shifts towards establishing permanent lunar bases, with plans for lunar colonization taking shape.

* **1981**: The Space Shuttle program launches, revolutionizing space travel with its reusable spacecraft.

* **1984**: International cooperation in space exploration begins to take shape, fostering collaboration between major powers.

* **1985**: The first human mission successfully lands on Mars, establishing a small colony and marking a major milestone in interplanetary exploration.

* **1980s-1990s**: Inspired by the ideas of Gerard O'Neill, large-scale orbital habitats gain traction. Ambitious plans are set in motion to construct self-sustaining space colonies in Earth's orbit.

* **2000s**: Lunar colonization efforts accelerate, with permanent bases established to serve as staging points for further space exploration.

* **2010s**: Mars colonization becomes a major focus, with significant progress made in establishing habitable environments and infrastructure.

* **2020s**: The first O'Neill-style space colonies, known as "O'Neill Cylinders," are completed in Earth's orbit. These massive habitats provide living space for thousands of people and facilitate further expansion into the solar system.

* **2030s**: Significant advancements in propulsion systems enable faster interplanetary travel, reducing transit times between celestial bodies.

* **2040s**: Terraforming efforts on Mars gain momentum, aiming to create a more habitable atmosphere and environment.

* **2050s**: Resource extraction from the asteroid belt and outer planets becomes increasingly viable, leading to the establishment of mining colonies and outposts.

* **2060s**: Floating cities in the upper atmosphere of Venus are constructed, utilizing advanced technology to withstand extreme conditions and conduct research.

* **2070s**: Space tourism becomes a popular industry, with commercial spaceports and luxury orbital resorts catering to travelers from Earth and beyond.

* **2080s**: The solar system is fully colonized, with thriving settlements on Luna, Mars, Venus, the asteroid belt, and the moons of Jupiter and Saturn.

* **2090**: The game's stage year, with humanity having achieved widespread colonization and interplanetary travel. Players take on roles as space-faring professionals, navigating the challenges and complexities of living in a diverse and politically complex solar system.

## Major Powers

* **European Federation:** Formed by the consolidation of European nations, the European Federation is known for its focus on scientific progress, sustainable development, and cultural diversity. They have a strong presence in both lunar and Martian colonization efforts.

* **North American Block:** Comprising the United States, Canada, and Mexico, the North American Block boasts advanced technology and a pioneering spirit. They have a significant influence on space exploration and maintain several major spaceports.

* **African Union:** United under the African Union, countries in Africa collaborate to participate in space exploration and capitalize on the resources available in the solar system. They focus on sustainable development and resource management.

* **Arabian Coalition**: The Arabian Coalition is composed of Middle Eastern nations that have made significant investments in space exploration, particularly in terraforming efforts on Mars and utilizing space-based solar power.

* **People's Republic of China:** China has become a key player in space exploration, emphasizing both scientific research and commercial interests. They have a significant presence on Luna, Mars, and the Belt, focusing on resource extraction and industrial development.

### Space Habitats

Space habitats are an integral part of the colonization efforts. Inspired by Gerard O'Neill's vision, orbital habitats are large rotating structures designed to simulate gravity and provide living space for large populations. These habitats are constructed using advanced materials and feature diverse ecosystems to support agriculture and provide a comfortable living environment. Modular design principles allow for expansion and adaptation as the population grows. The habitats serve as self-sustaining communities with their own life support systems, recreational areas, and commercial zones.

* Large structures designed to house human populations in space.

* Built with advanced materials that can withstand radiation and other hazards.

* Often designed with artificial gravity systems to simulate Earth's gravity.

* Powered by various energy sources, including solar panels and fusion reactors.

* Equipped with life support systems to provide air, water, and food for the inhabitants.

* Can include recreational areas, laboratories, and manufacturing facilities.

* May be connected to other habitats or spacecraft via airlocks or docking ports.

In the Sol space there are a few types of these settlements:

* **O'Neill Cylinder**: This is a cylindrical space habitat proposed by physicist Gerard O'Neill. It consists of two large cylinders, each 20 miles long and 5 miles in diameter, rotating around a common axis to simulate gravity. The interior of the cylinders can support a population of up to one million people.

* **Bernal Sphere**: This is a spherical space habitat proposed by physicist John Desmond Bernal. It consists of a hollow sphere with a diameter of about 1.6 km, rotating to simulate gravity. The interior can support a population of up to 10,000 people.

* **Stanford Torus**: This is a donut-shaped space habitat proposed by NASA in the 1970s. It consists of a large ring with a diameter of about 1.8 km, rotating to simulate gravity. The interior can support a population of up to 10,000 people.

* **Island Three**: This is a space habitat proposed by physicist Gerard O'Neill. It consists of a large sphere, about 1 km in diameter, with three smaller spheres attached to it. The interior can support a population of up to 10,000 people.

Strategic locations known as Lagrange Points have become home to remarkable space habitats, heralding a new era of human colonization and exploration.

Orbital lift systems, such as space elevators or mass drivers, are key components of the transportation infrastructure. Space elevators are immense structures extending from the surface of Earth or other celestial bodies into geostationary orbit, allowing for efficient and cost-effective transportation of people and cargo. Mass drivers use electromagnetic propulsion to launch payloads from planetary surfaces or from space to other destinations, reducing the dependence on traditional rocket-based launch systems.

* A type of space elevator that allows for easy transportation of people and cargo from the surface of a planet or moon to space.

* Consists of a long cable or ribbon that extends from the ground to a counterweight in space.

* Powered by various energy sources, including solar power and beamed power from the ground.

* Can be used to transport materials and people between the surface of a planet or moon and orbital habitats or spacecraft.

* Can be more efficient and cost-effective than traditional rocket-based launch systems.
