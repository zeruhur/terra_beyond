# Orion: Beyond the Frontier

The atmosphere within the *Radiant Horizon* was tense. As the ship cruised along the dim expanse of interstellar void, its hull bathed in the faint glow of a distant pulsar, the team assembled on the observation deck. Beyond the reinforced viewport, the nebulous silhouette of a derelict Precursor megastructure loomed—an ancient and colossal artifact orbiting a decaying star.

"Scans are picking up anomalies all over that thing," said Dr. Ilyan Jassir, the ship's Archivist. His voice quivered slightly, not from fear, but from awe. "This could predate the Ark of Precursor Knowledge by a million years. The energy signatures... They're impossible to categorize."

Captain Maia Veyron leaned against the console, her eyes narrowing. "And the radiation spikes? Are we safe to proceed?"

Ilyan hesitated. "With shielding at maximum and... no sudden movements, yes. But the structure itself is unstable—gravitational shifts could trap us if we're not careful."

At the center of the deck, Guardian Rook tightened his grip on his pulse rifle. "Let me guess. We're going in anyway." His deep voice carried a note of reluctant acceptance. 

"We didn't travel this far for a stellar light show," Maia replied curtly. She turned to Tamsin Corwin, the Tech-Scavenger. "Corwin, are you ready to breach the outer shell?"

Tamsin grinned, flipping her visor down. The intricate wiring of her exosuit glinted as it synced with her neural implants. "Always. If those ancient bastards left anything valuable behind, I’ll find it. You just keep the anomalies from frying my brain."

As the crew suited up, the *Radiant Horizon* shuddered slightly—a reminder of the unstable space around the artifact. Maia tapped her comm unit. "Horizon Pilot, keep the engines on standby. If this goes south, I want to be out of here in under five seconds."

"Copy that, Captain," replied Ezra Kael from the helm. "But fair warning: Subspace anomalies are making FTL calculations a nightmare. If we need to run, it'll be rough."

"Rough is better than dead," Maia replied before cutting the comm link.

The docking tube extended slowly, connecting the *Radiant Horizon* to the yawning maw of the Precursor megastructure. Through her visor, Maia watched as Tamsin moved first, her magnetic boots clanking against the structure's ancient surface. Dr. Jassir followed, his portable scanner flickering with strange data streams.

"This place..." Jassir whispered, his voice crackling over the comms. "It's not just a ruin. It's a graveyard."

Rook was the last to step through, his gaze scanning every shadow for movement. "Then let's not add our names to the list."

Inside, the crew found themselves in a vast, cathedral-like chamber. Walls of crystalline lattice hummed faintly, refracting the light from their helmets into a spectrum of alien hues. Glyphs pulsed softly, as if responding to their presence.

"These are neural relays," Jassir said, kneeling beside a glowing console. "The Precursors communicated directly with this structure. It’s... alive, in a way."

"Alive?" Tamsin muttered, her tone skeptical. "Great. Let's poke it and see if it bites."

Before anyone could respond, the chamber trembled. A low, resonant hum filled the air, and a section of the lattice shifted, forming what resembled a doorway.

"Captain..." Rook's voice was tight. "We’ve got movement."

Maia tightened her grip on her sidearm. "Stay sharp. Corwin, Jassir—analyze whatever you can, but be quick. We’re not staying longer than necessary."

The doorway flickered, and through it came a wave of heatless light. Within the radiance, shapes began to form—abstract at first, but gradually coalescing into something disturbingly humanoid.

"It’s a projection," Jassir stammered, stepping back. "Some kind of... interface."

The figure spoke, its voice reverberating like a choir from a distant star. "You tread on the bones of gods. Speak your purpose, travelers, or be unmade."

Maia stepped forward, her heart pounding. "We’re here to learn. To understand."

The projection paused, its form shifting into a kaleidoscope of shapes. "Understanding has a cost."

Tamsin's voice cut through the tension. "Yeah, well, let’s hope it’s payable in credits."

As the projection moved closer, its light began to pierce the crew’s suits, the glyphs on the walls glowing brighter. Maia felt a pressure in her mind—a presence sifting through her thoughts.

"Captain," Ezra’s voice crackled over the comm. "The anomaly’s destabilizing. We need to extract now!"

"Jassir, Corwin, move!" Maia barked, retreating toward the docking tube. But before they could leave, the projection extended a hand—solidifying into a crystalline shard. It floated toward Jassir, pulsing with unearthly energy.

"It’s... giving us something," he murmured, awestruck.

"Take it and run," Maia ordered.

As Jassir secured the shard, the chamber began to collapse, the walls warping inward as if the structure itself sought to consume them. The crew sprinted back to the *Radiant Horizon*, the airlock sealing just as the megastructure imploded into a singularity of light.

"Punch it, Ezra!" Maia yelled as the ship shuddered violently.

The *Radiant Horizon* shot into slipstream, narrowly escaping the singularity’s pull. In the silence that followed, the crew gathered on the deck, their breaths heavy.

Jassir held the shard up to the light, its crystalline surface shifting with an otherworldly glow. "This... this could change everything."

Maia nodded, her gaze fixed on the distant stars. "Or it could destroy us. Either way, it’s not the end—it’s just the beginning."
